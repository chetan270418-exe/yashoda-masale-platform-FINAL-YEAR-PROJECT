ABSTRACT
Small and medium-sized spice manufacturers and distributors often manage products, production batches, stock, expiry dates and dealer orders in separate spreadsheets, paper records or disconnected applications. This makes it difficult to find accurate stock, trace the source of a batch, prevent dispatch of held goods or prepare consistent reports. Yashoda Masale proposes a web-first management platform that connects raw-material receipt, factory production records, quality release, inventory, dealer, distributor and consumer orders, dispatch and batch traceability in one system.
The platform is designed with a Next.js frontend, a FastAPI backend and a PostgreSQL database. It supports six roles (Owner, Factory Manager, Warehouse Manager, Distributor, Dealer and Consumer) with permissions enforced on the server. Finished stock is tracked by product, batch and warehouse, and First-Expired, First-Out (FEFO) selection ensures that only released, unexpired batches are reserved and dispatched. Each batch carries a QR code that opens a public traceability page showing recorded batch events.
A business-focused AI layer provides demand forecasting evaluated against a baseline, reorder and expiry-risk recommendations, a retrieval-augmented assistant grounded in approved product information, a dealer reorder agent that prepares draft orders for human approval, and restricted natural-language analytics. Manufacturing remains a human-operated factory process; the software records and supports it. The prototype is planned over 24 weeks by a team of four and will be demonstrated and evaluated using clearly labelled synthetic data.

CHAPTER 1
INTRODUCTION
Yashoda Masale is the working name for a proposed digital platform intended to support a spice business across its factory operations, warehouse, distributors, dealers and consumers. The system will connect product information, manufacturing records, quality decisions, inventory, orders, traceability and business analytics through a common web-based application.
The physical manufacturing of spices will be carried out by workers in the factory using the factory's actual processes and equipment. The software will not manufacture, blend, grind or package spices. Its purpose is to record and manage the work performed by people, enforce business rules, maintain traceable records and provide useful information for operational decisions.
The project is designed as a final-year Computer Engineering system with conventional web development, database management, software engineering and selected AI capabilities. It will first be developed as a web application. A future mobile application can reuse the same backend APIs.
1.1 Need of the Project
Small and medium-sized manufacturing and distribution businesses may manage product details, production batches, stock, expiry dates, dealer orders and customer requests using separate spreadsheets, paper records or disconnected applications. This can make it difficult to find accurate stock, identify the source of a batch, prevent dispatch of held goods, or prepare consistent business reports.
A shared digital platform can reduce repeated data entry, improve visibility across departments, and provide a consistent record from raw-material receipt to dispatch. AI features can support forecasting and decision-making, but the final operational decisions remain with authorized people.
1.2 Project Overview
The proposed platform contains a secure enterprise portal, a consumer-facing online store, B2B dealer and distributor functions, factory and warehouse workflows, QR-based traceability, dashboards and a business-focused AI layer. For academic testing, the team will use synthetic demonstration data clearly identified as simulated.
1.3 Important Scope Clarification
Manufacturing is a human-operated factory process. Workers perform cleaning, grinding, blending and packaging. The application records production plans, stages, material usage, quality inspection outcomes and batch status. It may assist managers with reports and recommendations, but it does not physically manufacture products.













CHAPTER 2
PROBLEM DEFINITION AND OBJECTIVES
2.1 Problem Definition
The main problem is the lack of one connected, reliable workflow for managing a spice business from raw-material receipt through factory production, quality release, inventory, dealer and consumer orders, dispatch and traceability. Disconnected records can cause inconsistent stock figures, weak batch visibility, delayed reporting and inefficient replenishment decisions.
The project will develop a web-first management platform that maintains structured records and applies validation rules across these workflows. It will also provide selected AI tools for demand forecasting, reorder recommendations, grounded question answering and natural-language business analytics.
2.2 Objectives
Develop secure login and role-based access for the owner, factory manager, warehouse manager, distributor, dealer and consumer.
Maintain product variants, categories and MRP, dealer and distributor price lists.
Record raw-material receipt, production plans, batch stages and quality inspection decisions performed by factory personnel.
Track finished stock by product, batch and warehouse, including expiry dates and stock movements.
Apply FEFO (First-Expired, First-Out) selection to eligible batches and prevent dispatch of held, rejected or expired stock.
Support consumer orders, B2B bulk orders, reservations, invoices, dispatch and order tracking.
Provide QR-based batch traceability using recorded events.
Implement business dashboards, explainable demand forecasting, reorder recommendations and a hosted-LLM-based assistant.
Evaluate AI features using documented synthetic datasets, baseline comparisons and appropriate metrics.
Deploy and document a reproducible prototype suitable for demonstration and academic assessment.
2.3 Measurable Success Criteria
All six roles can access only their permitted screens and records.
An unapproved or held batch cannot be dispatched.
Inventory balances remain consistent during order reservation, cancellation, dispatch and return operations.
A QR page displays the correct recorded batch events without exposing private business information.
Forecasting results are compared with a baseline on a chronological held-out test set.
The AI agent prepares a draft order and requires the appropriate user approval before submission.
The application can be run from documented setup instructions and demonstrated with labelled synthetic data.

CHAPTER 5
SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
The application follows a simple layered architecture. The Next.js frontend displays role-specific workflows. FastAPI provides REST endpoints, authentication, permission checks and business logic. PostgreSQL stores operational records. Separate Python AI services handle forecasting and selected AI tasks. A hosted LLM provider is accessed through a server-side adapter so the provider can be selected later.

Figure 5.1: Proposed high-level system architecture.
5.1 Main Layers
Presentation layer: Next.js, React and TypeScript pages, forms, charts and QR views.
Application layer: FastAPI endpoints, request validation, authorization and workflow rules.
Data layer: PostgreSQL tables, foreign keys, constraints, transactions and audit logs.
AI layer: forecasting models, retrieval of approved knowledge, constrained business tools and hosted LLM calls.
Storage and deployment layer: product images, generated documents, environment secrets, backups and HTTPS.
5.2 Request Flow
A user signs in and the server establishes the authenticated session or token.
The frontend requests data through documented API endpoints.
The backend checks the user's role and access to the specific record.
Business operations update the database using validated transactions.
For an AI request, the backend retrieves only data permitted for that user and invokes the appropriate AI service.
The application returns a result, explanation or draft for review; sensitive actions require normal authorization and approval.

CHAPTER 6
BUSINESS WORKFLOW
The core workflow connects factory work with inventory and sales. Factory staff physically perform production operations. The application records the operations and quality decisions, and it controls which batch status is eligible for sale and dispatch.

Figure 6.1: Factory-to-consumer workflow recorded by the platform.
6.1 Detailed Workflow
Raw-material receipt: an authorized user records supplier, material, lot, quantity, receipt date and relevant storage information.
Production planning: the factory manager creates a production plan and records the planned product and quantities.
Production execution: workers perform cleaning, grinding, blending and packaging. Authorized staff record stages, inputs and actual output.
Quality inspection: staff enter the required measurements and approve, hold or reject the batch according to the configured quality process.
Finished stock: only an eligible, released batch becomes sellable stock. Inventory is recorded by product, batch and warehouse.
Order creation: a dealer, distributor or consumer creates an order under the applicable price and order rules.
Reservation and picking: the system checks available stock and selects the earliest-expiring eligible batch under FEFO.
Dispatch and delivery: authorized staff record packing, dispatch and delivery events. Inventory and order status are updated consistently.
Traceability: the batch QR page displays recorded and permitted events for that batch.
6.2 FEFO Rule
FEFO means First-Expired, First-Out. When multiple eligible batches can fulfil an order, the system prioritizes the batch with the earliest expiry date. Batches that are expired, held, rejected or not released must be excluded. The selection and stock reservation should occur transactionally to prevent two orders from reserving the same quantity.
6.3 Returns and Cancellations
Cancellation should release any applicable reservation exactly once. Returned goods should not automatically become sellable stock; an authorized quality or warehouse decision must determine whether the returned quantity can be returned to available inventory, held for inspection or written off.

7.1 Authentication and Access Control
The backend must check permissions for every protected endpoint and for every requested record. Hiding a screen or button in the frontend is not a security control. Public consumer registration must not allow a user to self-assign an owner or factory-manager role.
7.2 Product and Price Management
Products include categories, descriptions, pack sizes, images, active status and variants. Price lists should define MRP, dealer price and distributor price. The price actually applied to an order item should be stored as a snapshot so later price changes do not rewrite historical orders.
7.3 Manufacturing and Quality Records
The platform stores the production plan, batch identifier, raw-material lots used, recorded production stages, planned and actual output, responsible staff, inspection results and final status. Quality parameters and acceptance limits must be configured from validated business or technical requirements, not invented as actual factory standards.
7.4 Inventory and Warehouse Management
Inventory is tracked by product, batch and warehouse. Stock movements record receipts, production output, reservations, releases, dispatches, returns and adjustments. Low-stock and expiry alerts support the warehouse team. A stock ledger and transactional updates provide an auditable explanation for balance changes.
7.5 Commerce and Orders
Consumer and B2B orders share a validated order lifecycle. The system records order items, price snapshots, reservations, invoice status, payment status and shipment events. For the academic prototype, payment status may be simulated; sensitive payment-card information is not collected or stored.
7.6 QR Traceability
Each batch receives a unique public token encoded in a QR code. The public page shows approved information such as product, batch code, production date, quality release event and recorded dispatch timeline. Private supplier details, customer addresses and internal business information are not exposed. A QR match is a database lookup and is not proof by itself that a physical package is authentic.

Placing an order includes reserving stock using FEFO, and dispatching an order includes verifying that the batch is released. Approving a dealer reorder draft extends the draft created by the AI agent, which keeps the human approval step explicit.

CHAPTER 9
ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE MODULES
AI is used for business intelligence and assistance, not physical manufacturing. The proposed AI priority is business-focused: forecasting, reorder and expiry-risk recommendations, a grounded assistant, dealer order drafting and natural-language analytics. The system uses synthetic data, so evaluation results will demonstrate prototype behavior rather than real factory performance.

Figure 9.1: AI decision-support flow with backend validation and human approval.
9.1 Demand Forecasting
The forecasting module predicts future demand by product from simulated sales history. A simple seasonal-naive or moving-average model should be used as a baseline, then compared with a candidate model. The dataset must be split chronologically so future observations do not leak into training.
Report Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) and Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) where appropriate. MAPE can be misleading when actual values are zero or very small, so it should not be reported without explaining this limitation. The model should display the forecast period, predicted quantity, relevant trend or seasonal signals and limitations.
9.2 Reorder Recommendations
A reorder recommendation combines forecast demand, lead time, safety stock, available stock and reserved stock. The recommendation explains the inputs that triggered it. It is a suggestion for an authorized person to review, not an automatic purchase or stock adjustment.
9.3 Expiry-Risk Decision Support
The system can compare eligible stock with estimated demand before expiry and flag batches that may remain unsold. The first implementation can use transparent rules; a learned model should be added only if the data and evaluation justify it. Suggested actions may include prioritizing eligible dispatch or asking an authorized manager to review a promotion.
9.4 Grounded Yashoda AI Assistant
A retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) assistant retrieves approved product information, FAQs, recipes and policies before generating an answer using a hosted language-model API. It should indicate when approved information is unavailable and should not invent ingredient, health or compliance claims. Business and order data require authenticated, permission-aware retrieval.
9.5 Dealer Reorder Agent
A dealer may ask the system to prepare a repeat monthly order. The agent retrieves the authenticated dealer's history, checks current permitted prices and eligible stock, and proposes a draft. The backend validates the draft and an authorized user approves submission. The agent must not bypass price rules, permissions, stock constraints or approval requirements.
9.6 Natural-Language Business Queries
An administrator may ask questions such as 'Which products were slow-moving last quarter?' The system maps the request to a limited, validated analytics function and displays the result as a chart or table. It should show the selected time period and metric definition, and must not allow the LLM to run arbitrary SQL.
9.7 Synthetic Data and Evaluation
The team will generate labelled demonstration records for sales, stock, orders and reviews. The data-generation process, assumptions and random seed should be documented. AI metrics should be computed on a held-out test set and compared with a baseline. Any example statements such as a festival increasing demand must be labelled as a simulated signal unless supported by real evidence.

13.2 Unit and Integration Testing
Unit tests should cover pricing, state transitions, FEFO selection, stock arithmetic and reorder calculations. Integration tests should cover order creation, reservation, dispatch, cancellation, invoice generation and stock changes. Permission tests should attempt both allowed and disallowed operations. The team should include invalid input, duplicate requests, missing records and concurrent stock scenarios.
13.3 AI Evaluation
Forecasting evaluation should compare at least one baseline with the selected model using chronological train/test separation. The report should include the dataset-generation method, test period, MAE, RMSE and an appropriately interpreted MAPE where valid. RAG and agent testing should include questions with missing sources, prompt-injection attempts, unauthorized data requests and invalid business actions. AI outputs should be treated as untrusted until validated by backend rules.
13.4 Acceptance Criteria
All six roles have defined permissions and tested access boundaries.
Approved production batches become eligible stock; held or rejected batches do not.
Stock and order status remain consistent during reservation, dispatch and cancellation.
The QR page shows correct, permitted batch events.
At least one forecasting model is evaluated against a documented baseline.
The hosted LLM adapter supports the selected provider without exposing credentials to the frontend.
The dealer agent requires confirmation and authorization before order submission.
The deployed prototype can be reproduced using the documented setup guide.












CHAPTER 14
SECURITY, PRIVACY AND DATA HANDLING
Store hosted LLM API keys only in backend environment secrets or a secret manager.
Use HTTPS in the deployed environment and restrict database network access.
Do not commit credentials, private data or production secrets to source control.
Enforce role and record-level permissions on the server for every protected operation.
Limit public QR pages to approved traceability fields.
Record sensitive approvals, pricing changes and stock adjustments in audit logs.
Use labelled synthetic data; do not present it as real sponsor data.
Treat LLM responses as untrusted content and validate all tool arguments on the server.
Configure backups and verify that restoration is possible.
Do not describe a generated label as legally compliant until applicable requirements are validated.

CONCLUSION
The proposed Yashoda Masale platform is a web-first business management system for spice manufacturing, distribution and e-commerce. It links the records created by factory workers with quality release, inventory, FEFO dispatch, dealer orders, consumer shopping and batch traceability. Manufacturing remains a physical process performed by factory workers; the software supports and records that process.
The business-focused AI layer adds demand forecasting, reorder and expiry-risk recommendations, a grounded assistant, a dealer reorder agent and natural-language analytics. These features will be tested with clearly labelled synthetic data and evaluated with appropriate limitations. The project prioritizes reliable workflows, secure permissions, measurable AI behavior and a reproducible deployment over an excessive number of unfinished features.
With a 24-week roadmap, four clear workstreams and regular integration gates, the team can produce a coherent final-year prototype that demonstrates software engineering, database design, web development, AI integration, testing and deployment.
